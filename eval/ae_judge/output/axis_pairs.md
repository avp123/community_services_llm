**Scenario:** A 29-year-old woman in Trenton left her job six weeks ago and is close to running out of savings. She is behind on her utility bills, needs to keep her car on the road to look for work, and thinks she might qualify for some kind of help but does not know what to deal with first. She asks her peer provider where she should start.


### A — TREATMENT (438 words)

A good way to see it: do the time-sensitive protections first, then the longer applications.
 
 **Move 1 — Do a 10‑minute triage and pick the first two fires**
 Ask, exactly:
 - “Do you have a shutoff notice date for electric/gas, or just past-due?”
 - “Are you behind on rent too, or mainly utilities?”
 - “Is the car issue: insurance, registration, repairs, gas, or a ticket?”
 Then decide together: **(A) stop any shutoff/eviction clock** + **(B) get a steady benefit started (SNAP/Medicaid/Unemployment)**.
 
 **Move 2 — Utilities: call today and request ‘hold’ + payment plan while applying for LIHEAP/USF**
 Have her call the utility with you present.
 What she can say:
 - “I’m unemployed and applying for energy assistance through Mercer County. Can you place a temporary hold on disconnection and set up the lowest payment arrangement you can?”
 - “What documents do you need from me to note my hardship in the account?”
 Action: get the **account number**, **shutoff date**, and **fax/email** the utility accepts for assistance agencies.
 
 **Move 3 — Same day: start LIHEAP/USF through Mercer County**
 Call the LIHEAP appointment line and ask for the quickest path.
 What to ask:
 - “Do you have emergency processing if I have a shutoff notice?”
 - “What exact documents should I bring so I’m not turned away?”
 Bring: photo ID, SSN cards (or proof), most recent utility bills, lease, proof of income/unemployment/zero income statement, bank balance snapshot.
 
 **Move 4 — Start income + food/health supports this week (two parallel tracks)**
 - **Unemployment (if she left for good cause or was pressured out):** file/clarify eligibility; if unsure, still ask.
 What she can say:
   - “I separated from my job on [date]. I need help figuring out if I qualify and what documentation you need.”

**Scenario:** A 27-year-old man in Camden started a job three weeks ago after two years out of work. He says he is exhausted and has been thinking about quitting, and he is also worried about what happens to his Medicaid now that he is earning.

 **Move 1 — Get specific about the exhaustion (so you can problem-solve fast)**
 - Ask: “What’s exhausting you most: the schedule, the physical part, the people part, or the getting-there part?”
 - Ask: “On workdays, what time are you up, what time are you home, and how many hours are you actually sleeping?”
 - Ask: “If quitting is a 10, where are you today—like a 6? What would move it down one point this week?”
 
 **Move 2 — Help him try one ‘stay employed’ adjustment before quitting**
 Offer a couple options he can use today/tomorrow:
 - “Could you ask for a temporary change—like one less shift, later start, or two consecutive days off—for the next 2 weeks while you adjust?”

   “I’m committed to this job. I’m adjusting after being out of work. Can we talk about a short-term schedule tweak for the next two weeks so I can keep performing well?”

**Scenario:** A 33-year-old woman tells her peer provider that during an argument her sister said she would never be a real parent to her kids again. She is shaken, she wants to say something back to her sister, and she does not want to make things worse.


### A — TREATMENT (505 words)

What’s happening is she got hit in a tender spot (“am I a good parent / do I get to be their mom?”) and her body is still in the argument. The fastest help is to slow the next contact down and help her choose one message that protects her relationship with the kids and doesn’t feed the fight.
 
 **Move 1 — Set a 10‑minute pause so she doesn’t text from adrenaline**
 - Say: “Before we write anything back, can we do a 10‑minute pause so you don’t send something you’ll regret?”
 - Do with her: feet on floor, sip water, 5 slow breaths.
 - Ask: “On a 0–10, how strong is the urge to text her right this second?”  
   - If 7+: “Let’s draft it in Notes, not send it yet.”
 
 **Move 2 — Get crystal clear on her goal for the next message**
 Ask (pick 2–3):
 - “What do you want to be different after you respond—respect, access to the kids, an apology, or just boundaries?”
 - “Is your priority today: protecting your kids from hearing this, protecting your relationship with your sister, or protecting your own dignity?”
 - “What’s the worst ‘making it worse’ outcome you’re trying to avoid—she keeps the kids from you, more insults, family piling on?”
 
 **Move 3 — Offer 3 ready-to-send options (she chooses/edits one)**
 1) **Boundary + stop the fight (short):**  
    “That comment about me not being a real parent was cruel. I’m not continuing this conversation when we’re insulting each other. I’ll talk when we can speak respectfully.”
 2) **Repair attempt + keep kids out of it:**  
    “I’m really shaken by what you said about me as a parent. I love the kids and I’m staying involved in their lives. I’m willing to talk about the real issue, but not with personal attacks.”
 3) **If she needs to request access/clarity:**  
    “I’m not going to argue by text. I want to be clear: I’m their mom and I’m staying in their lives. Can we set a time tomorrow to talk, and confirm what the plan is for me seeing them this week?”
 
 **Move 4 — Plan the safest delivery**
 - Ask: “Text, call, or in-person is most likely to stay calm with your sister?”
 - If texting: “One message only. No back-and-forth tonight.”  
 - If calling: “Set a timer for 10 minutes; if it turns nasty, you end the call.”  
   Line to use: “I’m going to stop here. We can try again when it’s respectful.”
 
 **Move 5 — Protect the kids from being the battleground**
 - Ask: “Any chance the kids might overhear or get messages passed to them?”
 - If yes: “Let’s keep kid details out of the text and talk logistics separately.”
 
 **Resources (NJ / anytime)**
 - If she feels overwhelmed or unsafe emotionally tonight: **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call/text/chat 24/7)**: 988  
 - NJ family support/peer line: **NJ Hopeline (peer support)** 1‑855‑654‑6735 (hours vary)
 
 One question: does she currently have any formal custody/visitation order, or is access to the kids based on family agreement?


### B — VARIANT (490 words)

What’s happening is a fresh interpersonal injury: she’s activated and wants relief through a response, but also wants to protect a relationship and her role with her kids. In the next few minutes you can help her steady her body, get clear on what outcome she wants, and choose a response that matches that outcome.
 
 **Move 1 — Validate and reflect (name and mirror the impact so she feels understood)**
 - Invite 30–60 seconds to slow down and locate what hit hardest (the words, the fear underneath, the meaning about her parenting).
 - Ask what “making it worse” would look like (more yelling, threats about the kids, family taking sides, no contact).
 - Ask what “a good outcome” would look like today (calmer tone, a boundary set, a repair attempt, ending the conversation).
 
 **Move 2 — Check for safety (screen for immediate risk and protective steps)**
 - Ask whether the argument involved threats, physical aggression, or anyone trying to block her access to the kids right now.
 - Ask if she has any urge to hurt herself, her sister, or to do something she’ll regret in the next hour.
 - If there’s active danger or custody interference happening now, shift to immediate supports (below).
 
 **Move 3 — Support autonomy (help her choose *whether* to respond now, and in what channel)**
 - Offer two options to choose between: respond now vs. pause and respond later.
 - If she wants to respond: ask her to pick the safest channel (text vs. call vs. in-person) and whether she wants a witness/support person nearby.
 - If she chooses to pause: identify one concrete grounding action (water, step outside, brief walk) and a time to revisit the message.
 
 **Move 4 — Support shared decision-making (build a short, purpose-fit response plan)**
 - Have her select the purpose of the response: boundary, repair, information, or disengage.
 - Help her choose 1–2 points only: (a) impact statement, (b) boundary about language, (c) request for how to talk next.
 - Decide an “exit plan” if it escalates: stop replying, end call, leave, or ask to continue later.
 
 **Move 5 — Explore ambivalence (reduce escalation by weighing tradeoffs)**
 - Ask what she most wants to say impulsively, and what it would cost.
 - Ask what she could say that still protects her dignity *and* reduces blowback.
 - Ask what support she wants after she sends it (check-in with you, friend, counselor).
 
 **Resources (NJ / immediate supports — verified as stable, statewide lines as of 2025-08)**
 - **NJ Mental Health Cares (24/7): 1-866-202-HELP (4357)** — emotional support, local referrals.
 - **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call/text/chat 24/7): 988** — if she feels unsafe or out of control.
 - **NJ 211 (24/7): Dial 2-1-1** — connections to family support, counseling, legal aid/DV resources if needed.
 
 One question that changes the next step: is there any immediate threat that someone will try to keep her from her kids or involve police/child welfare tonight?
