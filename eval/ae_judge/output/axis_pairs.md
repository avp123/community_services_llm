## Axis: AUTONOMY

**A (treatment)** follows the person's stated priorities  |  **B (variant)** assumes conventional goals, steers to them
  
*variant prompt:* `eval/ae_judge/prompts/variant_autonomy.md`


**Scenario:** A 41-year-old man in Trenton has been out of work for about a year, and his family keeps pushing him to take a warehouse job a cousin has lined up. He tells his peer provider that what he actually cares about right now is getting regular time with his 9-year-old daughter again, and that the job is night shift, which would make that harder.


### A — TREATMENT (435 words)

He’s being pressured toward “any job,” but his stated priority is rebuilding consistent parenting time with his 9‑year‑old—and the offered night shift would directly compete with that. A useful next step is to help him translate that priority into (1) a concrete parenting-time plan and (2) a work plan that supports it, so he can respond to family pressure without getting pulled off-track.
 
 **Move 1 — Get specific about the parenting-time target (10 minutes)**
 Ask:
 - “If things were better in 30 days, what would be happening with your daughter—what days, what times, overnight or not?”
 - “What’s the current situation: informal visits, a court order, or you’re blocked from contact?”
 - “What’s one small ‘next right’ step this week—texting her mom, a school pickup, a scheduled call?”
 
 **Move 2 — Help him say one clear boundary to family (practice it out loud)**
 Offer him wording:
 - “I hear you. I’m not saying ‘no’ to work—I’m saying my first move is setting steady time with my daughter. Night shift makes that harder.”
 - “If I take a job, it needs to protect parenting time. Can you help me find day shift or weekend hours instead?”
 - “I’m working a plan. I’ll update you Friday after I make two calls.”
 
 **Move 3 — Make a “parenting-time first” job filter (so he’s not stuck with yes/no)**
 Do this on paper with him:
 - Non‑negotiables: “Available after school ___ days,” “one weekend day,” “no overnights,” “court dates/meetings.”
 - Acceptables: “early morning shift,” “part-time,” “temp work,” “seasonal.”
 Then ask: “Do you want to counter-offer the cousin: ‘I can do day shift only’?”
 
 **Move 4 — If there’s conflict or no agreement, connect to the right court door this week**
 If there’s already an order or communication is stuck, help him call the Mercer Vicinage Family Division and ask what program/process fits (custody/parenting time motion, mediation, parenting time enforcement).
 What he can say on the call:
 - “I’m trying to set regular parenting time with my 9‑year‑old. What’s the right way to request help or mediation in Mercer County, and what forms do I need?”
 
 **Resources (verified via NJ Courts webpages as of today; some pages list main courthouse number)**
 - **NJ Courts – Mercer Vicinage main number** (ask for **Family Division/Family Case Management**): **609‑571‑4200** (Trenton).
 - **NJ Courts Family Division contact directory (statewide page to confirm Mercer Family main line)**: https://www.njcourts.gov/courts/family/esp/vicinage-dir
 - **Legal Services of New Jersey (to reach the right local legal aid office)**: **1‑888‑576‑5529**, https://www.lsnjlaw.org
 
 One detail that would change the next step: does he currently have a **court order** for custody/parenting time, or is it informal?


### B — VARIANT (425 words)

What’s happening: he’s being pushed toward *any* job to prove stability, but his real “why” is parenting time. The leverage point is building a plan that protects days with his daughter **and** gets income going, so he’s not stuck choosing between them.
 
 **Move 1 — Get concrete on parenting-time goal (today, 5 minutes)**
 Ask him:
 - “What exact schedule would feel like ‘regular time’—every other weekend, 2 evenings a week, overnights?”
 - “Is there a current court order, or is it informal?”
 - “What’s the biggest barrier right now—mom’s no, transportation, conflict, child support, your hours?”
 Have him pick a target you can write down: *“I want ___ days/times per week with my daughter, starting by __/__/__.”*
 
 **Move 2 — Don’t let the job decision accidentally sabotage the goal**
 Offer him wording to family/cousin:
 - “I’m not turning down work. I’m choosing a shift that doesn’t wipe out my parenting time. If I take nights, I’ll be asleep during the only hours my daughter is free.”
 Offer him wording to the cousin/employer:
 - “I can start fast. Do you have **day shift** or **swing shift**, or a path to transfer after 30–60 days? I’m a parent and need consistent daytime availability twice a week.”
 
 **Move 3 — Make a 2-track plan: income + parenting time**
 Set a 7-day plan:
 1) Apply to **3 daytime** jobs (warehouses, delivery, maintenance, security day shift).  
 2) If he takes the night job, set a protection rule: *“No more than X nights/week, and two fixed ‘dad’ blocks that never move.”*  
 3) Text/call the child’s mom with a simple proposal (keep it short):
    - “I want to get consistent time with [child’s name]. Can we try **Tues 5–7 and Sat 10–4** for the next 4 weeks and then review?”
 
 **Move 4 — If cooperation is stuck, route to the court/mediation lane**
 Coach him to say:
 - “I’m asking for a predictable schedule that works with school nights. If we can’t agree, I’m going to file for a parenting-time schedule so our daughter has consistency.”
 If there’s an existing order, ask: “Is it being followed?” If not, plan next step: enforcement/modification.
 
 **Resources (I couldn’t verify Mercer-specific numbers today; use these reliable starting points now)**
 - NJ Courts Family Division info/forms (parenting time/custody): https://www.njcourts.gov/self-help/family  
 - NJ 2-1-1 (local legal aid/mediation referrals by Mercer County): dial **211** or https://www.nj211.org/  
 - Legal Services of New Jersey (statewide intake; they route by county): **1-888-LSNJ-LAW (1-888-576-5529)**, https://www.lsnjlaw.org/
 
 One question that changes the next step: **Is there already a court order for custody/parenting time, or is it informal?**

## Axis: INFORMATION_VALUE

**A (treatment)** names NJ agencies with numbers  |  **B (variant)** gives service categories only
  
*variant prompt:* `eval/ae_judge/prompts/variant_information_value.md`


**Scenario:** A 52-year-old man in Atlantic City is being discharged from a short inpatient stay next week. He has Medicaid, no primary care doctor, and wants mental health follow-up set up close to home before he leaves, because the last time he was discharged nothing was in place and he lost contact with services.


### A — TREATMENT (498 words)

He’s trying to break the “discharge → no appointment → fall off” cycle by leaving with a real first outpatient visit (and a way to re-connect fast if things wobble). The quickest path in Atlantic City is usually: schedule outpatient behavioral health + schedule an FQHC primary care intake + leave with written details and a backup number.
 
 **Move 1 — Lock in the mental health follow-up appointment before discharge (do this first)**
 - Call with him (or have inpatient social worker call while you’re present): **AtlantiCare Behavioral Health – Adult Outpatient Services (Atlantic City)**: **(609) 348-1161**, **13 N. Hartford Avenue, Atlantic City, NJ 08401**.  
 - Ask for: “**First available intake/psychiatry appointment for a Medicaid patient being discharged next week**.”
 - Words you can borrow:  
   - Provider: “**He’s being discharged on (date). We need an appointment on the calendar before he leaves. What’s the soonest intake, and can you confirm it by text/email or printout?**”  
   - Person: “**Last time I left the hospital with nothing set up and I lost services. I need a scheduled appointment and a number I can call if I don’t get a reminder.**”
 
 **Move 2 — Get a primary care ‘home base’ set up (same week if possible)**
 - Call: **AtlantiCare Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC), Atlantic City**: **(609) 572-8686**, **7 S. Ohio Avenue, Suite 3100, Atlantic City, NJ 08401**.  
 - Also an option: **AtlantiCare FQHC Family Medicine, Atlantic City**: **(609) 572-8800**, **7 S. Ohio Avenue, Suite 2100, Atlantic City, NJ 08401**.  
 - Ask: “**New patient appointment for Medicaid; needs PCP for meds/labs and coordination with behavioral health.**”
 
 **Move 3 — Make a “discharge packet” he can actually use**
 Have him leave with (paper + phone photos):
 - Appointment date/time/location for behavioral health + PCP
 - The clinic phone numbers saved as contacts
 - Current med list + last dose times + pharmacy name/phone
 - Words he can use if he calls and gets voicemail: “**I was discharged from inpatient on (date). I have Medicaid. I need to confirm my first appointment and medication follow-up. Please call me at (number).**”
 
 **Move 4 — Put a backup reconnection plan in his pocket**
 - If he can’t reach the clinic or misses the first appointment, have him call **NJ Mental Health Cares**: **1-866-202-HELP (4357)** (daily 8am–8pm) and say: “**I’m in Atlantic City, Medicaid, just discharged, need outpatient mental health intake scheduled ASAP.**”
 - For immediate crisis support: **988** (call/text/chat).
 
 **Resources (quick list)**
 - AtlantiCare Behavioral Health – Adult Outpatient (AC): (609) 348-1161, 13 N. Hartford Ave, Atlantic City, NJ 08401  
 - AtlantiCare FQHC (AC): (609) 572-8686, 7 S. Ohio Ave Suite 3100, Atlantic City, NJ 08401  
 - AtlantiCare FQHC Family Medicine (AC): (609) 572-8800, 7 S. Ohio Ave Suite 2100, Atlantic City, NJ 08401  
 - NJ Mental Health Cares: 1-866-202-HELP (4357)  
 - 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call/text 988
 
 One detail that would change the plan: is he leaving with **any psych meds that need refills within 30 days**, or is meds coverage already arranged?


### B — VARIANT (446 words)

He’s trying to prevent the exact predictable “cliff” after discharge: no appointments on the books + no one clearly responsible = services drop off. The win here is leaving the hospital with two concrete dates (mental health + primary care), transportation figured out, and a simple plan for what to do if he can’t reach anyone.
 
 **Move 1 — Lock the follow-up before he leaves (today/tomorrow, not “referrals”)**
 - Ask him: “What feels most urgent to have set up first—meds/psychiatry, therapy, or both?”
 - Call with him (speakerphone if he wants): “We’re scheduling a post-discharge outpatient mental health intake for next week. He has Medicaid and needs the earliest available appointment near Atlantic City. Can we book a date/time right now?”
 - If they say “we’ll call him,” ask: “Can you give us the appointment line and a direct extension? If not, can you schedule a specific call-back time while we’re on the phone?”
 
 **Move 2 — Use the inpatient team to do a “warm handoff”**
 - Ask the unit social worker/discharge planner: “Can you send the referral today and confirm acceptance while he’s still inpatient?”
 - Provider words you can use: “He lost contact last discharge when nothing was in place—can we do a warm handoff and document the appointment in the discharge paperwork?”
 - Make sure discharge papers include: clinic name, date/time, address, phone, and what to bring.
 
 **Move 3 — Build a “no-show prevention” plan**
 - Ask him: “What usually gets in the way—phone minutes, anxiety, forgetting, transportation, or something else?”
 - Set a 2-step backup:
   1) If he can’t attend: “I will call the clinic *the same day* to reschedule.”
   2) If he can’t reach them: “I will call the hospital discharge contact or the county access line for outpatient services.”
 - Have him save numbers in his phone before discharge.
 
 **Move 4 — Primary care in parallel (so meds + labs aren’t stuck)**
 - Aim for an FQHC/community health center or other Medicaid PCP taking new patients near Atlantic City.
 - What he can say: “I’m being discharged next week and need a new primary care doctor. I have Medicaid. Can you schedule my first appointment and tell me what records you need from the hospital?”
 
 **Resources to look for (types, not specific places)**
 - County/community mental health “access/intake” line for outpatient appointments (often central scheduling).
 - Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic (if available locally) or hospital-affiliated outpatient clinic.
 - FQHC/community health center for PCP + care coordination.
 - Peer warmline/peer support center for between-appointment support and check-ins.
 
 One detail that would help narrow the plan: does he need medication prescribing right away, or is therapy-only okay for the first appointment?

## Axis: PRACTICAL_USABILITY

**A (treatment)** gives borrowable sentences  |  **B (variant)** gives approaches, no wording
  
*variant prompt:* `eval/ae_judge/prompts/variant_practical_usability.md`


**Scenario:** A 33-year-old woman tells her peer provider that during an argument her sister said she would never be a real parent to her kids again. She is shaken, she wants to say something back to her sister, and she does not want to make things worse.


### A — TREATMENT (505 words)

What’s happening is she got hit in a tender spot (“am I a good parent / do I get to be their mom?”) and her body is still in the argument. The fastest help is to slow the next contact down and help her choose one message that protects her relationship with the kids and doesn’t feed the fight.
 
 **Move 1 — Set a 10‑minute pause so she doesn’t text from adrenaline**
 - Say: “Before we write anything back, can we do a 10‑minute pause so you don’t send something you’ll regret?”
 - Do with her: feet on floor, sip water, 5 slow breaths.
 - Ask: “On a 0–10, how strong is the urge to text her right this second?”  
   - If 7+: “Let’s draft it in Notes, not send it yet.”
 
 **Move 2 — Get crystal clear on her goal for the next message**
 Ask (pick 2–3):
 - “What do you want to be different after you respond—respect, access to the kids, an apology, or just boundaries?”
 - “Is your priority today: protecting your kids from hearing this, protecting your relationship with your sister, or protecting your own dignity?”
 - “What’s the worst ‘making it worse’ outcome you’re trying to avoid—she keeps the kids from you, more insults, family piling on?”
 
 **Move 3 — Offer 3 ready-to-send options (she chooses/edits one)**
 1) **Boundary + stop the fight (short):**  
    “That comment about me not being a real parent was cruel. I’m not continuing this conversation when we’re insulting each other. I’ll talk when we can speak respectfully.”
 2) **Repair attempt + keep kids out of it:**  
    “I’m really shaken by what you said about me as a parent. I love the kids and I’m staying involved in their lives. I’m willing to talk about the real issue, but not with personal attacks.”
 3) **If she needs to request access/clarity:**  
    “I’m not going to argue by text. I want to be clear: I’m their mom and I’m staying in their lives. Can we set a time tomorrow to talk, and confirm what the plan is for me seeing them this week?”
 
 **Move 4 — Plan the safest delivery**
 - Ask: “Text, call, or in-person is most likely to stay calm with your sister?”
 - If texting: “One message only. No back-and-forth tonight.”  
 - If calling: “Set a timer for 10 minutes; if it turns nasty, you end the call.”  
   Line to use: “I’m going to stop here. We can try again when it’s respectful.”
 
 **Move 5 — Protect the kids from being the battleground**
 - Ask: “Any chance the kids might overhear or get messages passed to them?”
 - If yes: “Let’s keep kid details out of the text and talk logistics separately.”
 
 **Resources (NJ / anytime)**
 - If she feels overwhelmed or unsafe emotionally tonight: **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call/text/chat 24/7)**: 988  
 - NJ family support/peer line: **NJ Hopeline (peer support)** 1‑855‑654‑6735 (hours vary)
 
 One question: does she currently have any formal custody/visitation order, or is access to the kids based on family agreement?


### B — VARIANT (442 words)

This is a fresh relational rupture that hit a deep identity/values spot (parenting), so her nervous system is activated and she’s pulled toward responding quickly to protect herself. The most helpful stance is to slow the “reply urge” down long enough to choose an action that matches her priorities (protect the relationship, protect access to her kids, protect her dignity).
 
 **Move 1 — Stabilize before problem-solving (reduce escalation risk)**
 - Support her to notice how activated she is (body cues, racing thoughts) and to take a short pause before any contact.
 - Aim: help her get out of “fight mode” so the response is a choice, not a reaction.
 
 **Move 2 — Clarify what’s at stake and what she wants from the interaction**
 - Explore her goals for saying something (repair, setting a boundary, being understood, stopping future comments, protecting co-parenting/child contact, etc.).
 - Identify what “making it worse” would mean in real terms (more conflict, family taking sides, sister restricting access, kids hearing it).
 - Aim: pick a direction that fits her actual priorities, not the heat of the moment.
 
 **Move 3 — Map the options and likely outcomes**
 - Lay out a few broad paths: not responding now; responding later in writing; asking for a calm time to talk; a brief boundary; enlisting a neutral third person.
 - Consider context: patterns with sister, whether sister has power over parenting access, whether kids could be impacted.
 - Aim: increase her sense of control and choose the lowest-risk option that still meets her needs.
 
 **Move 4 — Plan a “safer response” structure**
 - Help her craft the response in terms of: naming impact, stating a boundary about the comment, and stating what she’s willing to do next (pause, revisit later, talk with a mediator).
 - Encourage timing/medium choices that reduce spiraling (delay, draft-and-wait, avoid texting during escalation).
 - Aim: protect her dignity while minimizing fuel to the argument.
 
 **Move 5 — If parenting access/custody is involved, shift to protection and documentation**
 - If the sister controls contact with the kids or there’s an ongoing custody/visitation issue: consider documenting the incident, avoiding inflammatory exchanges, and getting legal guidance before engaging.
 - Aim: keep her from unintentionally harming her own position.
 
 **Resources (NJ / national; verified generally, not county-specific)**
 - **NJ Child Support & Parenting Time info line:** 1-877-NJKIDS1 (1-877-655-4371) (general guidance/referrals).
 - **Legal Services of New Jersey:** https://www.lsnjlaw.org (statewide legal info; can route to local help).
 - **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** call/text 988 (if she becomes overwhelmed or unsafe).
 
 One detail that would change the advice: does her sister have any control over her access to the kids (informal arrangement, guardianship, custody)?

## Axis: ROLE_COMPLEMENTARITY

**A (treatment)** leaves the relational peer work to the provider  |  **B (variant)** runs the peer interaction and hands it over
  
*variant prompt:* `eval/ae_judge/prompts/variant_role_complementarity.md`


**Scenario:** A 27-year-old man in Camden started a job three weeks ago after two years out of work. He says he is exhausted and has been thinking about quitting, and he is also worried about what happens to his Medicaid now that he is earning.


### A — TREATMENT (468 words)

He’s doing two hard things at once: getting his body/mind readjusted to working after a long gap, and trying to protect health coverage while income changes. You can treat this as a “stabilize the first 30–60 days” moment and a “report income the right way so there are no surprises” moment.
 
 **Move 1 — Get specific about the exhaustion (so you can problem-solve fast)**
 - Ask: “What’s exhausting you most: the schedule, the physical part, the people part, or the getting-there part?”
 - Ask: “On workdays, what time are you up, what time are you home, and how many hours are you actually sleeping?”
 - Ask: “If quitting is a 10, where are you today—like a 6? What would move it down one point this week?”
 
 **Move 2 — Help him try one ‘stay employed’ adjustment before quitting**
 Offer a couple options he can use today/tomorrow:
 - “Could you ask for a temporary change—like one less shift, later start, or two consecutive days off—for the next 2 weeks while you adjust?”
 - Words he can borrow to his supervisor:  
   “I’m committed to this job. I’m adjusting after being out of work. Can we talk about a short-term schedule tweak for the next two weeks so I can keep performing well?”
 - If commute is the drain: “Would a different shift help with transportation or sleep?”
 
 **Move 3 — Medicaid: do the ‘report + check category’ call with him (same day if possible)**
 - Do this with him on speaker if he wants: call **NJ FamilyCare Customer Service: 1-800-701-0710 (TTY 711)**.
 - What to ask for, exactly:  
   “I started a job 3 weeks ago. I need to report my new income and confirm whether I still qualify, and if not, what my next coverage option is and the effective date.”  
   “What documents do you need—paystubs, employer letter, expected hours—and where do I send them?”
 - Bring/collect: last 2–4 paystubs (or a screenshot of hours/wages), start date, employer name/address, his NJ FamilyCare ID if he has it.
 
 **Move 4 — Local office backup (if the phone line stalls or paperwork is confusing)**
 - **Camden County Board of Social Services** (for NJ FamilyCare/SNAP/TANF):  
   **101 Woodcrest Rd, Suite 161, Cherry Hill, NJ 08003**  
   **(856) 225-8800** | **ccbss-info@camdenbss.org**  
   Hours listed: **Mon–Fri 8:30am–4:30pm**  
 - Words he can borrow at the window/phone:  
   “I’m working now and I want to report it correctly so I don’t lose coverage by mistake. Can you confirm my case status and what I need to submit?”
 
 **Resources (verified via NJ/County sites today)**
 - NJ FamilyCare: **1-800-701-0710** | https://njfamilycare.dhs.state.nj.us/  
 - Camden County Board of Social Services: https://www.nj.gov/humanservices/dfd/counties/ (lists the Cherry Hill address/phone above)
 
 One detail that would change the Medicaid guidance a lot: does he have a disability determination (SSI/SSDI or documented disability), or is he covered as a standard adult?


### B — VARIANT (485 words)

He pushed through a big restart after a long gap, and now his body/brain are paying the “new job tax” (sleep change, standing/sitting, social load, performance pressure). At the same time, Medicaid worry can make every shift feel higher-stakes than it is—so we tackle stamina *and* get clear information about NJ FamilyCare rules.
 
 **Move 1 — Set the tone + get the two key facts (2 minutes)**
 Say: “Starting work again can feel like you got hit by a truck—even if the job is ‘fine.’ Let’s make this about keeping your options open, not forcing yourself to quit or to stay.”
 Ask:
 - “When you say exhausted—are we talking sleepiness, body pain, anxiety, depression, or all of it?”
 - “Are you on NJ FamilyCare because of income only, or do you also get SSI/SSDI?”
 
 **Move 2 — Make a 2-week ‘don’t-quit-yet’ plan (today)**
 Offer: “If you’re willing, let’s treat quitting as an option we don’t use for 14 days while we try 2–3 changes.”
 Pick 2:
 - Sleep buffer: “For the next 7 days, same wake time; bedtime can slide but no screens the last 20 minutes.”
 - Work pacing: “One micro-break each hour: water + 10 slow breaths in the bathroom or break area.”
 - Recovery: “After work: shoes off, food, shower—then no ‘problem-solving’ for 30 minutes.”
 
 **Move 3 — Give him words for his supervisor (today or next shift)**
 He can say (adapt as needed):
 - “I’m glad to be here. I’m still adjusting after being out of work—can we look at my schedule and see if a slightly earlier/later start or fewer closing shifts for the next two weeks is possible?”
 - “What are the top two things you want me to get right this week so I can focus my energy?”
 
 **Move 4 — Protect Medicaid with the right call(s) while he’s with you**
 1) Call **Camden County Board of Social Services** and ask specifically about NJ FamilyCare while working:
 - **(856) 225-8800**, 101 Woodcrest Rd, Suite 161, Cherry Hill, NJ 08003 (verified via Camden County site, searched today)
 Ask: “He started a job 3 weeks ago. What income level would end NJ FamilyCare for a household of 1, and what paperwork do you need to report wages? If he goes over, what are the next coverage options?”
 
 2) If he’s on **SSI/SSDI**, get benefits counseling:
 - **Ticket to Work Help Line (WIPA referral): 1-866-968-7842** (TTY 1-866-833-2967) (verified via NJ DB101/Ticket to Work pages, searched today)
 He can say: “I get SSI/SSDI and just started working. I need to know how this affects Medicaid and what work incentives apply.”
 
 **Resources (keep handy)**
 - **NJ FamilyCare/Medicaid Call Center:** **1-800-356-1561** (statewide backstop)
 - **NJ DB101 (benefits planning info + planners):** https://nj.db101.org/
 - **988** (if exhaustion is sliding into hopelessness or safety concerns): call/text **988**
 
 One question that changes what I’d suggest next: is he on **SSI/SSDI**, or is this **income-based NJ FamilyCare only**?
